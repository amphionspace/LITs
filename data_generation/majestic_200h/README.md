The link at `/119010446/tts-assets/data_generation/majestic_200h_20260913` points to it.

The user revised training quotas on September 14 to **50 hours Chinese, 25 hours English and 25 hours mixed**, counted from unique accepted recordings. The new training run is `ljs_majestic_100h_backbone21k_20260914`; the latest user decision reuses the 21k acoustic backbone, resetting speaker rows, Adam, and the step counter while keeping the LR recipe unchanged.

`pipeline.sqlite` is authoritative. `pipeline_progress.json` reports accepted recordings and hours separately for each language/split. `supervisor_heartbeat.json`, `workers.json`, `servers.json` and `logs/` identify running components. Accepted audio and portable manifests are under `accepted/`.

- Existing Emilia2 real-speech transcripts: `scan_emilia_text.py` reads only metadata from the already prepared local training manifest, excludes recordings used in the local Emilia validation sets, and stops after approximately 200 text-rate hours of mixed candidates. `import_emilia_text.py` tokenizes with the actual LITs frontend and removes historical/pool duplicates by normalized text and phonemes. It contains 10,510 rows, 10,507 normalized unique texts, and 10,213 rows containing both Chinese and English. The associated DOTA-ME-CS paper explains that scripts were generated by GPT-4o and manually checked before human recording. The user explicitly approved this existing reviewed source on September 13. `import_reviewed_csv.py` admitted **4,997 training-only texts**, approximately 6.095 text-rate hours, after content/frontend/duplicate checks. No audio was downloaded and original audio hours are unknown. Repository revision, checksum and provenance findings are saved beside the CSV. These rows still undergo near-duplicate checks before synthesis and full audio QC afterward.

No new LLM generation, rewriting or concatenation is used. Previously generated, manually reviewed DOTA scripts are the explicitly approved exception to real-transcript sourcing. Near-duplicate screening against historical and newly admitted text runs before synthesis admission (FTS retrieval plus normalized edit/containment checks; this is not a semantic-equivalence guarantee).

## Running pipeline

`start_pipeline.py` holds an exclusive lock, waits for all four local VoxCPM2 services, and launches:

1. CPU text admission and best-candidate selection.
2. Asynchronous synthesis against GPU 0/1/2/3 services on ports 8200/8201/8202/8203, eight requests per service.
3. Qwen3-ASR sharing GPU 2, with no reference transcript in the ASR context.
4. WavLM and CAMPPlus sharing GPU 3; DNSMOS ONNX models sharing GPU 0. DNSMOS feature preparation remains on CPU.

Two candidates are scored per round. Following the user's request to generate additional candidates where needed, the current per-text caps are 4 for Chinese, 4 for English, and 8 for mixed text. English was reduced from 12 to 4 on September 14 after the approved reference showed low marginal yield in late retry rounds; each round still scores two candidates. Only the best fully scored passing candidate is accepted. QC includes content, speaker similarity, DNSMOS, duration, clipping, silence and MAS length gates from `config.json`; failures are retained with reasons. The 24/48 kHz outputs are written atomically. One accepted audio file per text is hard-linked into `accepted/`.

Active languages are read dynamically from `config.json`. New pool rows can be imported while synthesis continues. Supervisor restarts recover unfinished candidate states after confirming previous workers have exited. Candidate IDs/seeds are persistent. An infrastructure failure stops workers and records the error instead of counting incomplete audio as accepted.

On September 14 the user prioritized Chinese and mixed synthesis, temporarily pausing English. After the reference-4 pilot, the user approved sample 4 as the English quality reference too, and English production resumed. All three languages are active, with each GPU's eight preferred request slots allocated **3 Chinese / 3 mixed / 2 English**. An idle slot can serve another active language. Under the revised 50/25/25 quotas, Chinese and mixed have reached their targets, so new work is primarily English. Retries stop once the corresponding split/language quota is met; active backlog is bounded.

Listening sample 4 (candidate 72003) is configured under `synthesis_references.en` with its exact transcript, and under `quality_reference_audio_by_language.en` following the user's explicit approval. Chinese/mixed synthesis and speaker QC retain the original Chinese reference. All numeric gates remain unchanged. New candidates record synthesis reference path/transcript/SHA-256; new metrics record quality reference path/SHA-256. Previous accepted English recordings retain their original passing QC. The final dataset audit permits the original reference and the approved per-language reference and verifies recorded hashes.

The completed paired reference-4 pilot generated 36 candidates per reference on 18 short/medium/long texts. Against the selected English anchor, new-reference synthesis passed 18/36 versus 9/36 using the Chinese synthesis reference, with average WavLM/CAMPPlus 0.829/0.805 versus 0.541/0.699. Against the original Chinese anchor, the two arms passed 0/36 and 1/36 respectively. These are different quality definitions, not an improvement under the same Chinese identity gate. Pilot audio and both comparisons are under `reports/english_reference4_probe/`, without training quota credit.

The completed one-time English test rescore queued a bounded 512 existing test recordings (one per text) for rechecking against the newly approved English anchor to address the exhausted test pool. Previous candidate records are preserved in `candidate_quality_history`. Reports group production quality by both synthesis and QC reference, so newly generated English and rescored old English remain distinguishable.

The rescore recovered 72 recordings but left a small test deficit, so English test texts received one additional two-candidate round (`max_candidates_per_text_by_split.test.en=14`; English train/val were then capped at 12). The English test quota is now met. Subsequently all English split caps were reduced to 4; existing accepted results are preserved.

The completed DNSMOS benchmark compared CPU and CUDA DNSMOS on the same 24 recordings with eight worker threads and concurrent speaker scoring. Across three warm repeats, mean batch time was 4.744 s on CPU versus 1.544 s on CUDA. Maximum score difference was 1.23e-6; all sampled DNSMOS gate decisions matched. Results are in `reports/dnsmos_benchmark/`. Production uses ONNX Runtime GPU 1.23.2 from an isolated runtime path, requires a real CUDA provider, disables TF32 for these ONNX sessions, and records provider/timing information. The metrics process sees physical GPUs `[3,0]`: speaker models use logical device 0, DNSMOS uses logical device 1. This microbenchmark speedup is not an end-to-end collection speed guarantee.

The first English pilot had a very low pass rate, primarily on speaker similarity. A 12-text, paired-seed comparison using an accepted English training utterance as the prompt did not improve full-gate acceptance (0/24 in both arms), so the original Chinese reference and all thresholds were retained. Results are in `reports/english_reference_probe/comparison.json`. Speaker similarity and ASR scores do not establish native accent quality; the 50-hour English target is not guaranteed by the available text volume.

## Training handoff

After **all nine language/split quotas** are met and outstanding candidates finish, the supervisor releases its synthesis services, then runs `prepare_training.py`, `training/stage2/preflight.py`, and the stage-two launcher. The audit rechecks uniqueness, historical exclusions, group separation, every selected QC result, WAV headers and MAS length compatibility. Any failed audit/preflight prevents training.

The current recipe combines original LJSpeech (speaker 0, 21.05h) with the new 100h MajesticVoice collection (speaker 1). It loads only the 21k Stage 1 acoustic backbone, resets both speaker embeddings, and creates fresh Adam/scheduler state at step 0. It preserves the source Mel normalization. All modules train jointly with duration/prior/flow weights 1:1:1; the Conv duration head and constrained online MAS remain unchanged. No A/B training is launched.

Unique utterances are naturally sampled (final target/LJS row shares 82.31%/17.69%). The final training recipe uses a peak LR of 3e-4 for all groups, 1000-step warmup, a stable phase through 80% of the budget, then linear decay to 2e-5 over the final 20%. The frozen 65,282-row manifest gives 340 updates per epoch and 170,000 updates over 500 epochs. The existing handoff entry points dispatch on `training.mode=backbone_init`. Full data audit and GPU preflight remain required. Exact checkpoints are retained at 1k for smoke evaluation, 2k for full evaluation, then every 5k, with per-speaker/language duration diagnostics and 650-text generation metrics. See [the complete joint training recipe](../../training/majestic_scratch/README.md).

## Background watchdog

`watchdog.py` runs independently with its own exclusive lock. Every 30 seconds it checks service/process health; every five minutes it writes `reports/supervisor_status.json` and the Chinese `SUPERVISION.md` in the dataset root. Reports distinguish generated candidates from unique accepted audio and provide quota deficits, source availability, per-language rejection reasons and proposed remedies. Each failed owned component has a limit of three automatic restarts per hour. It does not lower quality thresholds, automatically change candidate limits, overwrite training checkpoints, or restart generation during/after training handoff. Unresponsive live services and orphaned GPU children require inspection rather than blindly launching competing replicas.

`supervision_control.json` controls `enabled`, `auto_recover`, and `pause_generation`. `watchdog_state.json` records its PID and heartbeat. See [TRAINING_PLAN.md](/ai_sds_wuzz/DATA_TTS/MajesticVoice_200h_20260913/TRAINING_PLAN.md) for the full Chinese training and operations instructions.

`audit_distribution.py` produces `reports/text_distribution_audit.json`. It compares accepted training texts with accepted plus retry-exhausted training texts, excluding pending attempts from both denominators. Dimensions include text length bin, source, English word count and language switches. This measures observed filtering bias, not semantic/topic coverage or whether the original pool matches the intended deployment distribution.

One-time reference probes, rescore/benchmark scripts and temporary tests were removed after verification. Their reports remain in the dataset root. Text import, exclusion, quota and QC code stays because it builds or audits the current training data.
